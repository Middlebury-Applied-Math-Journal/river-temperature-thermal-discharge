# Predicting River Temperature and Risk from Power Plant Thermal Discharge

Thermal discharge from power plants can raise downstream river temperatures and stress aquatic ecosystems, especially during warm seasons. While limits on the amount of allowed heat output do exist, they are not defined dynamically to account for varying river conditions. This study uses a one dimensional advection-di!usion-cooling model to predict how excess heat moves from a nuclear power plant through downstream transport, spreading, and atmospheric cooling. By comparing winter, spring, summer, and fall cases, the model examines whether the same permitted discharge limits produce di!erent thermal impacts under di!erent seasonal river conditions. The analysis includes time-dependent predictions, a steady-state decay length for plume persistence, and a population model with temperature-dependent mortality to connect temperature changes to ecological risk.
